How We Test AI Website Builders

This page explains the evaluation framework used to organise reviews. It is not a claim that every builder has completed an identical controlled test: the evidence basis is stated on each review, and scores should be treated as provisional editorial assessments where comparable test records are not published.

By The Tech GUY · Methodology and evidence standards

1. Evidence basis and independence

We do not claim that every reviewed product was tested on a paid subscription. A review may draw on first-hand use, official documentation, current public pricing and independent secondary sources. Each page should identify its evidence basis and disclose material gaps. Affiliate relationships are described in our disclosure; commercial links must not be treated as evidence that a feature works.

2. How to interpret hands-on claims

We describe a product as hands-on tested only when the specific use is supported by a real project or contemporaneous notes. A strong future test record should include the prompt, plan and region, test date, project goal, edits required, publishing constraints, screenshots and any repeatable measurements. Claims about speed or Core Web Vitals require comparable measurements with the tool, device, location, test method and date recorded. Where those records are unavailable, we use research-based language instead.

3. The scoring rubric

CategoryWeightWhat we measure
Ease of use20%Time from signup to published site; decisions forced on the user; help resources
AI capability25%Quality of generated copy/layouts/images; how much editing output actually needs
Design quality20%Default output polish; typography and spacing; animation and interaction options
Features & ecosystem15%Business tools, e-commerce, CMS, integrations, portability
Value for money10%Price vs capability at each tier; free-plan usefulness; renewal fairness
Performance & SEO10%Page speed on real published sites; meta/structured-data controls; indexing basics

The weights above define a framework, not proof of a completed controlled experiment. Scores should be read as editorial judgments rather than statistically precise measurements. Before describing a score as reproducible, the review must publish the category-level scores, evidence for each category, the weighted calculation and any missing-data treatment. A score above 9.0 must never be described as a recommendation without reservation; every product has trade-offs.

4. We re-verify continuously

AI builder features and pricing change frequently and may vary by country, currency, billing period or promotion. Date-stamp a claim only after checking the provider’s official source. A quarterly schedule is an editorial target, not a promise that every claim has already been re-verified. Material changes should be noted only after they are confirmed.

5. What we don't do

  • We don't accept payment for placement or scores.
  • We don't publish reviews of tools we haven't used hands-on.
  • We don't hide negatives to protect affiliate income — every review, including our #1 pick, lists real cons.
  • We don't use vendor-supplied screenshots as substitutes for our own testing notes.

6. Corrections policy

If you spot a factual error, please report it through the public project issue tracker. Please do not post private or sensitive information in a public issue.
